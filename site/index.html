I The argument

A Mustang burbles. A Ferrari shrieks. Both are 90° V8s, and I wanted to know why they sound nothing alike: not well enough to describe it, well enough to point at the part. And to find out whether that sound falls out of the geometry, or has to be put there by hand.

The usual answer is that the American engine fires unevenly. It does not. Both fire once every 90° of rotation, eight times a cycle, perfectly spaced. At the flywheel the two keep identical time.

The part is the crankshaft. Seen from the nose, its four throws make either a cross or a line.

Here are two cycles of it, slowed until they can be counted: the whole engine, and then what each manifold gets of it. Listen to the engine, then to one bank alone.

The engine ticks evenly. Alone, a bank coughs twice in quick succession, waits three quarters of a turn, and coughs again. Now fit the flat crank and listen to one bank again.

On the flat crank a bank alone is as even as the whole engine. Nothing limps, so nothing burbles. Same block, same heads, same cam.

III The instruments

The engine is a sealed mechanism that turns and gets hot, and knows nothing about being observed. Six instruments are bolted to it afterward, and none of them may reach into the physics to make its own job easier.

The indicator card

Cylinder pressure against volume, for one cycle of one cylinder: the engine drawing its own picture, as Watt’s did. Wide open, and then throttled, to the same scale.

The dynamometer

Close, and wrong in a way that can be named: the curve is too broad, with torque peaking about 400 rpm low and power 600 high. The missing part is intake runner tuning.

The inspection sheet

Every number the project quotes is checked there, each against something outside the project: a derivative against finite differences, a cylinder head against a flow bench, a firing order against the casting, the thesis against a Fourier transform.

IV What is not in it

An unstated simplification is a lie, and a stated one is a design decision. The two largest pieces of work in this project are not in it. A nonlinear gas solver, verified against Sod’s shock tube to one part in 10⁵, made header length worth 14% of torque, and collapsed the difference between the two crankshafts, which is the one measurement the project exists to make. It was taken out.

A limitation nobody attempted is a guess. A limitation somebody built, measured and reverted is a result, and both are written down.

What all of this left behind is an engine that turns over in a terminal and cannot move anything, burning fuel that does not exist, and doing all of that correctly.
